How it works, and what it does to your file

Each page is rendered to a canvas at a resolution capped by your DPI setting, re-encoded as a JPEG at a chosen quality, and written into a new PDF. When you ask for an exact size, the same process runs repeatedly with a binary search over quality and scale until the result fits.

The trade-off: pages become images. The file gets substantially smaller and looks the same at normal reading size, but text is no longer selectable or searchable, and links, forms and bookmarks are lost. That is the right trade for a scan, a portfolio, or a document you need to email. It is the wrong trade for a contract someone needs to search or a form someone needs to fill in.

If your PDF is mostly text and already small, this tool may make it larger. It will tell you if that happens rather than handing you a worse file.

Why it runs in your browser

PDFs are one of the most sensitive things people compress — contracts, passports, medical records, bank statements. Every free online compressor requires you to upload all of that to a stranger's server. There is no technical reason for it. Everything here happens locally, which you can verify by opening your browser's network tab and watching nothing leave.
